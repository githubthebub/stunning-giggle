# THE MASTER PLAN
*Musk × Galloway — for the channel built inside a game*

**The mission, one sentence:** Become the only Pokémon fiction channel on YouTube where every frame of every story is real, reproducible gameplay from a fan game the audience can open in a browser tab — then make the audience's own dreams the content.

Most Pokémon lore channels are speculation factories: screenshots of games they can't touch, theories they can't test. We own the game. That's not an advantage. That's a different physics.

The flagship isn't a video. It's a save file that keeps producing sequels.

## Part 2 — The Format Ladder & Shorts Flywheel

One universe, four formats, each feeding the next rung:

1. **Iceberg documentaries** (13–15 min) — the trust engine. Forensics, experiments, receipts.
2. **Character fics** (8–12 min) — the heart engine. Start with the wholesome POV (7.72): *"I'm a Lv 55 Darmanitan. My Trainer Can't Beat Route 1."* — the best title in the field, and the sandbagged gym rematch is verifiably filmable on the Champion preset.
3. **Shorts** — every longform yields 3+ self-contained proofs. The tile-overlay reveal is a 40-second Short *by design*. The wipe experiment is another. The guaranteed-catch demo is another. Each Short ends on the same frame: the title screen with "Continue as Champion" highlighted, and a link.
4. **Community verification videos** — viewers reproduce our experiments; we compile, credit, and canonize their findings. Their comments become our B-roll.

The flywheel: docs build trust → fics convert trust to love → Shorts convert love to reach → reach produces reproducers → reproducers produce evidence → evidence feeds the next doc.

## Part 3 — The Community Crossover Endgame

Here's the moat nobody can cross: **in this game, a dream serializes to pasteable text.** Any viewer can export their Dream World as a Dream Link code. So in Phase 3, we open submissions — and viewers' actual dreams become canon episode settings. We walk the white bridge into *their* save, live, on camera, peer-to-peer. The episode "Someone Left a Light On in Dream #4471" is filmed inside a subscriber's real dream, with their real befriended Pokémon dozing in their real Entree Forest.

No other channel can copy this. Not because they lack the idea — because they lack the object. Copying us requires shipping a playable game where dreams are portable data. The endgame: the channel's canon is co-authored by its audience, one pasted code at a time. Lore that viewers *live in* before we film it.

## The Galloway Playbook

### Packaging-first pipeline
**No script gets written until the title and thumbnail are approved.** Order of operations: (1) draft 15 titles, (2) mock 3 thumbnails at actual feed size (~320px), (3) kill everything that doesn't survive the squint test, (4) lock the package, (5) *then* write the script as a promissory note the package signed. The flagship followed this: "remembers a journey nobody took" is paid off concretely in Beat 8 with the Alder fingerprints. **No clickbait debt. Ever.** Debt gets collected in AVD.

### The 10 Packaging Rules
1. **The title is a story, not a topic.** "This Pokémon Game Remembers a Journey Nobody Took" — subject, mystery, stakes, eleven words.
2. **Thumbnail asks; title answers — or vice versa. Never both, never neither.** "WHO PLAYED IT?" + the title = one complete open loop.
3. **One emotion per package.** Ours: quiet dread wrapped in wonder. A cozy Game Boy world lit like a crime scene.
4. **Three elements max in the thumbnail.** Tree, tiny sprite, menu box. Delete the fourth.
5. **Numbers beat adjectives.** "Lv 55 Darmanitan," "eight layers," "(9,11)" — specificity is credibility.
6. **The package must contradict itself.** A finished save nobody finished. A champion who can't beat Route 1. Contradiction is the click.
7. **Test at 320 pixels or don't test at all.** If the thumbnail reads as a green blur at feed size (see the tearjerker's original packaging), it lost before upload.
8. **Counter-program the feed.** When the niche saturates on dread, ship the wholesome Darmanitan. When it saturates on cozy, ship the crime scene.
9. **Every claim in the package must be filmable.** The dark-AU concept (6.70) had the best drama and died because Team Plasma isn't in the repo. Filmability is a packaging rule, not a production note.
10. **If the package needs the video explained to it, kill the package.** Not the video — the package. Then usually the video.

### Hook doctrine (first 30 seconds)
Press the button **live** — no logo, no "hey guys," no context. Show the impossible object inside 10 seconds (badge case, 99,999 money, five HMs). State the anomaly by 20 (a Pokédex that remembers battles you never fought). **Pre-sell the ending by 30**: "Eight layers down, I can finally tell you what the Entralink actually is." The hook is a mortgage on the finale — the viewer now must finish to collect.

### Retention doctrine
- **Open-loop spacing:** never more than 90 seconds without an unanswered question. The iceberg format enforces this structurally — every layer boundary closes one loop and opens a darker one. Structure beats willpower.
- **Re-hook marks at 30% / 55% / 80%:** the tuck-in payoff at ~3:00, the wipe experiment at ~7:00 (placed exactly where documentary retention curves sag — a deliberate mid-video spike, we *delete our own save on camera*), the tile overlay at ~12:00.
- **Payoff before loop, always.** Viewers tolerate a new question only in the exhale after an answer.

### 90-day launch cadence
**Weeks 1–2:** Film flagship (all 10 beats), lock packaging for Episodes 2–3. **Week 3:** Flagship live + tile-overlay Short (day 2) + wipe-experiment Short (day 5). **Week 4:** Guaranteed-catch Short; pin the reproduction challenge comment ("wipe your save — tell me what's still in the forest"). **Weeks 5–6:** Episode 2 (tearjerker refit) + 2 Shorts. **Weeks 7–8:** Episode 3 (creepypasta, save actually planted) + 2 Shorts. **Weeks 9–10:** Darmanitan POV fic + counter-programmed wholesome Shorts. **Week 11:** Community verification compilation — viewer evidence, credited. **Week 12:** Announce Dream Link submissions. **Week 13:** First community-dream episode. Cadence law: **one longform every 14 days, two Shorts every week, zero missed slots.** Consistency is the algorithm's love language.

## The Production Engine — The Repo IS the Studio

- **Run the set:** `npm start` in `/home/user/stunning-giggle` → `http://127.0.0.1:4173`. Zero dependencies, zero build step. The set boots in one second.
- **Capture:** browser at 1080p+, OBS window capture. Overworld, full 17-type battle engine, the Entralink pull-in, the Entree tree tuck-in — all real, all first-take-able. Saves persist in the browser, so scenes are re-shootable from any state.
- **Dream scenes:** `/dreamworld.html` runs the Dream World standalone — film the Island of Dreams, berry garden, and dream house without touching the RPG save.
- **State control:** `game/save.js` holds the New Adventure / Champion presets — instant access to any story state. `game/maps.js` is the forensics department: the (9,11) coordinates live there for the overlay shot.
- **The horror filter is built in:** sprites render as cute procedural art offline and upgrade to official artwork online. So for horror beats, **cut the network**. The "dream-degraded" look — familiar creatures rendered *almost* right — is our signature visual for anything inside a dying dream. An accident of engineering, promoted to cinematography.

## Analytics Doctrine — Three Numbers, Nothing Else

1. **CTR** — did the package work? Under 4% at 48 hours: swap thumbnail; still under 4% at day 7: retitle. The video is never "underrated." The package failed.
2. **AVD** — did the structure work? Target 55%+ on longform. Every dip in the retention graph gets a timestamp autopsy against the beat sheet.
3. **Returning viewers** — is this a channel or a slot machine? If a format rung doesn't grow returning viewers across 3 uploads, the rung dies. The channel doesn't.

**Kill criteria:** any format that misses 2 of 3 targets twice consecutively is dead — no eulogy, no "give it one more shot." **The Musk rule — delete the part:** the best segment is no segment. If viewers skip it, it isn't misunderstood; it's *wrong*. Delete it from the format template, not just the video. Never defend a segment. Segments are not people. And the corollary: if you haven't deleted a beat from the template in 90 days, you aren't looking at the graphs hard enough.
