# 02 — The Shorts Playbook

Shorts are the discovery engine: the algorithm tests every short on a seed
audience regardless of channel size, which is exactly why a 1k-sub channel can
compete. The two signals that matter most: **viewed-vs-swiped ≥75%** (the first
2 seconds) and **average-percentage-viewed, ideally >100%** via loops (the last
2 seconds). Everything below is engineered for those two moments.

## The Flagship Short (video #1 — shoot this first)

**"This Charizard survived a washing machine."** (~15s)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:01.5 | Extreme close-up: the mangled card FILLS the frame. No intro, no face, no logo. VO + on-screen text: *"This Charizard survived a washing machine."* The damage is the hook — it defies every pristine-slab thumbnail in the niche. |
| 0:01.5–0:04 | Phone lifts into frame, card in live camera view. The OCR scanline sweeps; recognized text types itself out: *"CHARIZARD… found."* The magic trick is watching real software read a real ruined object. |
| 0:04–0:07 | Dex entry slams in: artwork, the cry plays, stats scroll. The Dex (deadpan narrator voice, your Starter Squad register): *"…I've seen things."* |
| 0:07–0:10 | BALL THROW at Charizard's real capture rate (45). Shake… shake… **BURST OPEN.** On-screen: *"It broke free?!"* — a genuine failure state the audience learns is real, not scripted. |
| 0:10–0:13 | Throw #2. Three shakes… CLICK. Confetti, chiptune fanfare, counter ticks: **Registered: 1/1025.** |
| 0:13–0:15 | Hard cut to the battered card on the table — then the *next* card slides in and the VO starts the next hook mid-sentence before a hard cut to black. |

**Loop engineering (upgrade from the judge panel):** don't just frame-match the
ending — **loop into the hook**. End every short by *starting* the next scan
mid-sentence, then hard-cut so the replay lands on frame one. The rewatch then
replays the hook itself, pushing APV past 100% — the strongest ranking signal.

**Pinned comment (every short):** curiosity gap + link:
*"Your card works too. Even fakes — the scanner roasts those. Watch me scan an
entire $50 bulk lot →"* + native **Related Video** link to the current long-form
episode. (Related Video is the highest-leverage native funnel tool; re-point it
weekly at the newest episode so it never goes stale.)

## The Eight Formats (rotate to fight novelty decay)

Rotation matters: the scan-trick alone fatigues. Each format re-skins the same
15-second engine with a different emotional key.

### 1. Washing Machine Survivors (Damaged Card Amnesty)
- **Hook:** frame 1 is always the wound. *"My dog ate half this Eevee."*
- **Why:** direct inversion of grading culture — the dex literally cannot see
  condition, so the "worthless" card gets the identical ceremony as a slab.
  Endowment effect: viewers re-value the beat-up cards they already own.
- **Titles:** "This card survived a house fire. The Pokédex doesn't care." /
  "My dog ate half this Eevee. Still counts." / "PSA would grade this a 1.
  The dex grades it CAUGHT."

### 2. Fake Card Court
- **Hook:** *"This card is fake. Let's see if the scanner notices."* Verdict withheld.
- **Why:** binary-verdict structure = the best completion engine available —
  viewers physically cannot leave before the ruling. The OCR reading gibberish
  ("Chorizard") aloud is the punchline. Occasionally a good fake *passes* — which
  is its own episode. Schadenfreude aimed at the counterfeit, never at a kid.
- **Titles:** "The $3 'Pikachu Illustrator' from Wish vs. my scanner" /
  "Fake Card Court, Case 12: the People vs. 'Chorizard'"

### 3. The Quarter-Machine Dex
- **Hook:** price sticker first, always. A 25¢ yard-sale sticker in macro.
- **Why:** Trahan's constraint-as-format. Anchoring: the famous dollar figures
  make 25 cents a headline. The anti-scalper emotional core.
- **Titles:** "25¢ yard-sale card gets the full Pokédex ceremony" /
  "$16,492,000 card and this 25¢ card get the same dex entry" (sunset the LP
  framing by month 3 per the rules of engagement)

### 4. The Shiny Lottery (Day-N streak — capped)
- **Hook:** *"Day 31 of scanning cards until the Pokédex gives me a shiny (1/64)."*
- **Why:** purest variable-ratio loop on the platform, and the RNG is provably
  real. Day-N serialization manufactures return viewers.
- **Discipline:** cap streaks at ~45 days per season and pre-bank episodes
  (see [04-90-day-plan.md](04-90-day-plan.md)) — a solo daily streak with no
  buffer is a burnout contract; two sick weeks must never kill the format.
- **Titles:** "Day 17: THE BALL BURST OPEN AGAIN" / "Day 44: it finally happened."

### 5. Binder Resurrection
- **Hook:** a binder creaks open under the camera. *"This binder hasn't been
  opened since 4th grade."*
- **Why:** nostalgia for *your own objects*, not the brand's. Generation effect:
  every viewer involuntarily asks "what's in MY old binder?" — comments become
  the submission queue. Start with your own childhood binder; move to
  viewer-submitted binder *photos* (digital submissions first — no mail
  logistics, no minors-mailing-property problem).
- **Graft (from the judge panel):** name each registered Pokémon after the viewer
  who submitted it. Later (~5k subs), permadeath "Death Reports" mourn losses
  in-character — endowment + loss aversion at maximum power, held back until the
  audience exists.
- **Titles:** "Scanning my childhood binder, page 1: the crown jewel is a Rattata" /
  "A subscriber's kid picked these 9 cards. Full ceremony for all of them."

### 6. The Dex Reacts (Starter Squad heritage, upgraded to a character)
- **Hook:** card scanned → before the throw, the Dex (or the Pokémon, in your
  parody voice) reacts to its own card, price, or condition.
- **Why:** the one asset no scanner app or PokéTuber can copy. Promote the voice
  from cameo to **persistent named character by video 3** — the sarcastic Dex
  narrator is a brand asset, and (crucially) it is Nintendo-proof IP that
  survives any takedown.
- **Titles:** "Scanned a Charmander. He has opinions." / "Bulbasaur reacts to
  being worth 12 cents."

### 7. Speedrun the Engine (the Nintendo-proof pillar)
- **Hook:** *"Adding a working Pokédex scanner to a browser in 60 seconds."*
  Screen-capture dev shorts: how the OCR reads a bent card, how capture rates
  work, how shake checks roll.
- **Why:** 100% legally bulletproof (your code, your commentary), recruits a
  dev-curious second audience that de-risks the channel, and builds the
  AM2R-style career hedge. Short-form devlogs are the format's current life
  (3–4/week short updates reportedly outperform monthly polished devlogs).
- **Titles:** "How my scanner reads a card my dog chewed" / "The math that
  decides if the ball breaks open"

### 8. You Commented, I Coded (graft from the judge panel)
- **Hook:** cold-open on a screenshot of a real comment. *"You asked for
  flashlight mode. Built it in 48 hours."*
- **Why:** the cheapest IKEA-effect engine available: commenting becomes a free
  lottery ticket, which manufactures comment volume (an algorithmic signal)
  and visible builder credibility. Hard 48-hour build cap; keep a public
  "not this season" list so scope stays sane.

## Packaging rules

- **Titles carry numbers or verdicts**, never adjectives: prices, day counts,
  case numbers, X/1025. ("Numeric-stakes hooks" are the one Logan Paul mechanic
  worth stealing wholesale.)
- **Thumbnails/first-frames:** the object, macro, damaged — never your face,
  never UI. The wound is the pattern interrupt in a feed of pristine slabs.
- **On-screen HUD on every video:** `Registered: x/1025` + `Dex fund: $xx.xx`.
  Burned in, always. The HUD is the season's silent narrator.
- **5th-grade scripting, ~15–30s lengths.** Sub-30s needs near-100% retention;
  don't pad.
- **Metadata hygiene:** "Pokédex"/"Pokémon" never in the channel name or handle
  (see [05-legal-survival.md](05-legal-survival.md)). In-video speech is fine.
- **Every short gets:** the Related Video link (current episode), the pinned
  curiosity-gap comment, and a Discord/email pointer in the description.

## Cadence

- **Growth phase (months 1–3):** 1–2 shorts/day. Sustainable because production
  is batched: **one scan-session shoot yields a week of atomic shorts** — the
  cadence is an editing problem, not a shooting problem.
- **Steady state:** 3–5 shorts/week + 1 long-form/week.
- **Small-audience register (months 1–3):** talk to the actual room size as a
  running bit — *"all twelve of you came back for Day 9, and I respect that"* —
  it converts the 200-view phase from demoralizing to charming, and it means the
  format degrades gracefully if virality is slow.
