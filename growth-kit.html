        Poké Mart — Making It Pay

        Climb in order. Each rung funds the next, and the ladder ends with something you own.

        IP reality check

        Pokémon belongs to Nintendo, Creatures, GAME FREAK, and The Pokémon Company. Fan animation has historically been tolerated, but any monetized fan video can be claimed or struck at any time. Fully original animation, art, and voice work is safer than reusing game sprites, footage, or music — and even then, nothing fan-made is truly safe. Treat ad revenue as a bonus, never the plan, and use the audience to launch things you own.

        0–1K subs

        Grind phase

        No money here and that's fine — retention is the only currency. Target: 70% of viewers still watching at 30 seconds. Every checklist item above exists to buy you this.

        1K subs

        YouTube Partner Program

        Turn on ads knowing fan content gets claimed. Whatever trickles through is pizza money — celebrate it, don't budget around it.

        500 true fans

        Patreon / memberships

        Sell access to you, not the IP: early episodes, votes on the next twist, names in the credits, behind-the-scenes writing streams. A hundred fans at $3 beats a million claimed views.

        Any size

        Commissions

        The channel is a public portfolio. Sell what it proves you can do: voice acting, script writing, custom fanfic commissions, editing for other creators.

        Endgame

        Original series

        Introduce one original character you fully own, let the audience fall for them, then spin them into their own series. Merch, sponsors, and real licensing only ever attach to what's yours. Audience first — then own the IP.
