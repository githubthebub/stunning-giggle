# Episode 1 — "Entry Zero"

**Runtime target:** 9:30–10:30 · **Format:** narrated fanfic, VHS-archive visual style
**Retention plan:** hook ≤ 15s · first open loop by 0:45 · pattern break every ~90s · emotional peak at 7:20 · cliffhanger + CTA in final 40s

> Visual language: animated stills with slow parallax, Pokédex UI overlays,
> timecode burns, tape grain. Narrator (M.) voice: dry, close-mic, deposition
> calm. Music: single low piano figure, silence used as punctuation.

---

## COLD OPEN — 0:00–0:20 (the hook)

**[BLACK SCREEN. Timecode burn: 20:00:00.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Every Pokédex ever issued has a trainer ID. The Kanto registry
lists ten thousand and forty-one of them. I checked twice.

**[CUT: a battered red Pokédex on a desk, screen cracked, lit by a lamp.]**

**M. (V.O.):** The one on my desk is number zero. It doesn't exist. And last
night, at exactly eight PM… it turned itself on.

**[The dex screen flickers ON. Hard cut to title card: THE LOST POKÉDEX — tape-warped, one frame of static. SFX: tracking clunk.]**

---

## ACT 1 — THE STORAGE UNIT — 0:20–2:10

**[B-roll: storage facility hallway, roll-up door, dust in lamplight.]**

**M. (V.O.):** My name doesn't matter. What matters is that for three years I
was a lab aide at Professor Oak's research facility in Pallet Town, and my job
was to scan paper into computers so the paper could be thrown away. When the
lab was decommissioned, the estate mailed me a key and one sentence: *unit 44,
contents yours, thank you for your service.*

**M. (V.O.):** Most of unit 44 was exactly what you'd expect. Filing cabinets.
Dead monitors. A coffee mug I'm fairly sure I lost in 2019.

**[PATTERN BREAK — 0:58. Music stops. Close-up: a steel document box. Label, handwritten, Oak's writing:]**

**M. (V.O.):** And then, under the desk it had clearly been *hidden* beneath,
a locked steel box with a label in the Professor's own handwriting. Three
words.

**[INSERT: label fills frame — "DO NOT ARCHIVE."]**

**M. (V.O.):** Not "do not open." Not "confidential." *Do not archive.* I
digitized that man's files for three years. Archiving was the whole religion of
that lab. Everything was archived. Everything… except this.

**[The box opens. Inside: the Pokédex, a dead simple-cell battery, and a folded photo — face down.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Inside was a first-generation Pokédex, a battery that had leaked
sometime in the last decade, and a photograph, folded once, face down. I want
to be honest with you about the photograph, because everything that follows
depends on it. **[beat]** It's ruined. Water damage. The face is gone. All you
can make out is that someone stood outdoors, on a hill, and that the sky behind
them was full of birds.

**[OPEN LOOP #1 planted. The photo is set aside, leaning against the lamp — it stays in frame for the rest of the series.]**

---

## ACT 2 — THE DEVICE — 2:10–4:20

**M. (V.O.):** A dead Pokédex is not remarkable. I collect them. So I did what
I always do: new cell, contact cleaner, hold the power button.

**[Montage, quick cuts: battery swap, button press. Nothing. Again. Nothing.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Nothing. For six days, nothing. On the seventh day I was at my
desk at eight PM, filing taxes — I need you to understand how little I was
asking for a ghost story that night — when I heard the startup chime from
inside a closed drawer.

**[SFX: muffled first-gen dex chime. PATTERN BREAK — 2:55: first UI shot. The screen renders:]**

```
POKÉDEX v1.04 — PROPERTY OF INDIGO LEAGUE
TRAINER ID: 000
ENTRIES: 151 — CORRUPTED: 151
CLOCK: 20:14 — [date: twenty years ago today]
```

**M. (V.O.):** Trainer ID zero-zero-zero. The League help line told me IDs
start at one, and then asked which collector shop had sold me the *replica*.
One hundred fifty-one entries — all corrupted. And the clock. The clock
doesn't tick. It shows fourteen minutes past eight… on a date twenty years
ago. To the day.

**[OPEN LOOP #2: freeze-frame on the clock. Red circle nothing — just hold the shot. Trust the audience.]**

**M. (V.O.):** It stayed on for exactly fourteen minutes. Eight to eight-
fourteen. Then it died, and no button on this earth would wake it until eight
PM the next night. It has done this every night for five weeks. I've stopped
filing taxes.

**[Beat of dry humor = likability spike. Comment seeders love this line.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Corrupted files can be restored. That was my actual job, once.
So each night, fourteen minutes at a time, I started rebuilding Entry 105.
Why 105? Because it was the least damaged. I've since wondered whether it was
the least damaged because the device *wanted* it read first.

---

## ACT 3 — THE OFFICIAL STORY — 4:20–5:30

**[Visual: the official Pokédex entry rendered clean and clinical, like a museum placard.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Entry 105. Cubone. You know this one — everyone knows this one.
The official entry reads: *"It wears the skull of its deceased mother. When it
becomes lonesome, it is said to cry loudly."*

**M. (V.O.):** The saddest Pokémon in the dex. That's the official story, and
it's been the official story for twenty-five years.

**[PATTERN BREAK — 5:05. The clean placard glitches. The restored entry renders in a different font — handwriting-adjacent, personal. Music out entirely.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Here is what Trainer 000's Pokédex says instead. I'm going to
read it to you exactly as it restored. I haven't edited a word.

---

## ACT 4 — THE RESTORED ENTRY — 5:30–7:50 (emotional core)

**[Full screen: the entry text, revealed line by line as M. reads. Slow push-in. No music until noted.]**

**M. (V.O., reading, flat:)** *"Entry 105. Cubone. Caught — no. Met. October
ninth, north of Lavender.*

*"The official entry is wrong about mine. Everyone in that town said the same
thing: it wears its mother's skull, it cries at the moon, it will not evolve,
it is broken. People kept using that word. Broken.*

*"It took me three weeks to learn the truth. The skull it wears isn't hers.
It's borrowed — pulled from the ruins of the old tower after the Rocket raid
scattered every Marowak den on the hill. My Cubone never saw its mother die.
It saw her driven off, and it found a skull in the ash, and it put the story
on like a helmet. Because a dead mother you can grieve. A missing one you have
to keep waiting for.*

*"And it would not evolve. Not in eleven battles. Not at the Plateau. The
machine says evolution is triggered by experience. It isn't. It's triggered by
letting go. Evolving into Marowak meant wearing that skull forever. It meant
agreeing she was gone. My Cubone was not broken. It was faithful."*

**[Beat. M.'s voice changes — barely. First crack in the deposition calm.]**

**M. (V.O., reading:)** *"October thirtieth. Update to entry.*

*"Today, in the burned den line north of the tower, we found her.*

*"I will not describe the sound it made when it took the skull off, because
there isn't a word for it, and I refuse to give it the wrong one. It set the
skull down in the ash, face up, the way you'd return something you'd borrowed.
And then my Cubone — bare-faced, six years old, smaller than I'd ever realized
— walked to its mother in the plain moonlight, where anyone could see it.*

*"The official dex will tell you what a Pokémon is. It will never tell you who
one is. That's what this dex is for."*

**[MUSIC RETURNS — single warm piano note, 7:20, exactly on "who one is." This is the clip line. The screen shows:]**

```
ENTRY RESTORED. 1 OF 151.
```

**M. (V.O.):** Entry restored. One… of one hundred fifty-one.

---

## ACT 5 — THE CLIFFHANGER — 7:50–9:40

**[Back to the desk. Night. The dex screen glows. Timecode: 20:13:41.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Two things happened when the restoration finished, and I need
to show you both, because I've watched this footage forty times.

**M. (V.O.):** First. The trainer ID photo — the corrupted one on the device,
not the paper one — repaired itself. Not all of it. A sliver. The top left
corner. **[INSERT: the static image; one corner resolves.]** It's a piece of
something red and white. Everyone I've shown it to sees a different thing. A
League cap. A Pokéball. A Poké Center sign. I genuinely don't know.

**[OPEN LOOP #3. Hold four full seconds of silence on the image. Comment bait does the work.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Second. At eight-fourteen, when the screen died… it didn't die
all the way. For a single frame — I had to step through the footage to catch
it — the entry list scrolled on its own. Past 106. Past 143. It stopped on
Entry 25. Pikachu.

**[INSERT: freeze-frame. Entry 25 — PIKACHU. File size: 44.1 MB.]**

**M. (V.O.):** Every corrupted entry on this device is a few kilobytes of
text. Entry 25 is forty-four megabytes. Text doesn't weigh that much.
**[beat]** Audio does.

**M. (V.O.):** The restoration takes about a week per entry. I've already
started this one. Next week, at eight PM, I'm going to press play — and
whatever is on that recording, you'll hear it the same moment I do.

**[TITLE CARD: THE LOST POKÉDEX. Under it, small: ENTRY 2 — "THE RECORDING."]**

**M. (V.O.):** Until then, one question, and I want wrong answers only:
**whose Pokédex is this?** The comment with the best theory gets read at the
top of the next episode. Entry restored. Goodnight.

**[END CARD — 9:40–10:00: subscribe end-screen framed as a dex UI: "TRACK RESTORATION: 1/151". Loop the corner-of-photo image behind it.]**
