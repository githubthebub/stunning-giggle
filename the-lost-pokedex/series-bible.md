# The Lost Pokédex — Series Bible

## Logline

A former lab aide inherits a storage unit from Professor Oak's decommissioned
lab and finds a Pokédex registered to Trainer ID 000 — an ID that was never
issued. The device only powers on for fourteen minutes a night, its clock is
frozen twenty years in the past, and its entries aren't encyclopedia text.
They're field notes. Someone *knew* these Pokémon. Restoring the entries slowly
un-corrupts the trainer's ID photo — and the closer it gets to finished, the
clearer it becomes that this journey was erased on purpose.

## The Narrator

**"M."** — never fully named on camera. Worked as an aide in Oak's lab during
its final years, mostly digitizing paper records. Practical, dry, a little
tired; starts the series as a skeptic annoyed at being haunted by office
equipment and ends it as the only person willing to finish the restoration.
M. narrates in first person, past tense, like a deposition that keeps slipping
into a ghost story.

Voice rules:

- Short declarative sentences when scared. Longer ones when moved.
- Never says "creepy," "scary," or "haunted." The audience supplies those.
- Reads restored entries verbatim, flatly — the entries carry the emotion.

## The Device

- **Powers on only from 8:00 PM to 8:14 PM.** No explanation until Episode 9.
- **Clock frozen** at 8:14 PM, twenty years ago to the day the series starts.
- **151 entries, all corrupted.** Restoring one takes M. about a week of work.
- **Trainer ID photo:** corrupted to static. Each restored entry repairs ~0.7%
  of the image. The audience watches it resolve across the season.
- **Entry 25 — "PIKACHU"** is the only entry with an audio recording attached.
  This is the season's mid-point bomb.

## Canon rules (what keeps the fanbase instead of angering it)

1. **Never contradict game/anime canon — reframe it.** The official dex entry
   is always quoted accurately first. The lost entry then reveals the story
   *behind* the official line. Canon is the setup; the fanfic is the payoff.
2. **The dex entries are personal.** Official: species-level facts. Lost:
   one specific individual Pokémon this trainer actually met.
3. **Wholesome-dark, PG-13.** Loss, grief, and dread are fine; gore and cruelty
   on screen are not. Every dark setup earns a warm resolution or a humane one.
4. **The mystery is fair-play.** Every reveal is foreshadowed at least two
   episodes early. Rewatch value = clip value = free promotion.

## Recurring motifs

- **8:14** appears somewhere in every episode (timestamps, receipts, route signs).
- **The Gastly** is the audience's emotional barometer — it reacts before M. does.
- Every restored entry ends with the same line, read flat:
  *"Entry restored. [N] of 151."* — the series' signature/clip-line.

## Tone references

Found-audio mystery pacing, nature-documentary warmth for the Pokémon stories,
and VHS-archive visual language (tracking lines, timecode burns, dex UI overlays).
