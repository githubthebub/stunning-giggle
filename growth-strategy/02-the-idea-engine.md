# 02 — The Idea Engine

> The idea is 80% of the video. The title and thumbnail are 15%.
> The video itself is 5% — and it's the only part most creators work on.

Views on YouTube come from exactly one equation:

```
Views = Impressions × CTR × (Retention → the algorithm grants more impressions)
```

You control CTR with the **idea + title + thumbnail** (the "packaging") and
retention with the video. This file is the packaging machine. Run it before
every upload, no exceptions.

## Rule zero: packaging before production

**You do not film anything until the title and thumbnail exist and you would
click them.** Written down, mocked up, shown to three people who don't love
you. If the packaging isn't exciting, the idea is dead — no amount of editing
resurrects a video nobody clicks.

This inverts how amateurs work (film something → "what should I title this?").
The title isn't a label for the video. **The video is a fulfillment of the
title.**

## Step 1 — Outlier research (where ideas come from)

Ideas are not brainstormed from nothing. They're found in data:

1. Build a list of 30–50 channels: your niche, adjacent niches, and 5–10
   giant general channels.
2. For each, find the **outliers**: videos doing 5–20x that channel's median
   views. Median, not average — one viral hit skews averages.
3. Log every outlier in the [idea bank](templates/idea-bank.md) with: what the
   *concept* was (not the topic), why it clicked, and how recent it is.
4. Special weight to outliers on **small channels**. A 50K-sub channel with a
   4M-view video means the *idea* did that, not the brand. That idea is
   underpriced.

Do this 2 hours a week, forever. Every great channel is running this loop
whether they admit it or not.

## Step 2 — The remix formula

Never copy, never invent from zero. Remix:

```
Proven format (from outlier research)
  + One fresh twist (scale, stakes, setting, constraint, or person)
  + Your unfair advantage (skill, access, personality, or resources)
  = Your video
```

Examples of the twist axes:
- **Scale:** "I built a treehouse" → "I built a 5-story treehouse"
- **Stakes:** "Trying X" → "If I fail X, I delete my channel"
- **Constraint:** "I cooked dinner" → "I cooked dinner for $1"
- **Setting:** "Surviving 24h" → "Surviving 24h in an abandoned mall"
- **Person:** "Pro plays chess" → "Pro plays chess against 100 kids"

A pure copy caps at ~10% of the original's views. A remix can beat the
original — the algorithm sees a fresh concept, viewers see a familiar promise
with a new payoff.

## Step 3 — Volume, then brutal selection

- Generate **30–50 ideas per video you'll actually make.** Most will be bad.
  That's the point — you can't pick a 1-in-50 idea from a list of 3.
- Score each idea 1–10 on one question only: **"Would a stranger who has never
  heard of me click this?"** Not "is this good content." Not "am I proud of
  it." Would. They. Click.
- Only ideas scoring 8+ get a title and thumbnail mock. Only mocks that
  survive the three-stranger test get filmed.

## Step 4 — Titles

- **Under 60 characters.** Front-load the hook — mobile truncates.
- The title states a **specific, visual, high-tension promise**:
  - Weak: "My trip to Japan" → Strong: "I Survived 7 Days in Japan on $10"
  - Weak: "Chess tips" → Strong: "This Move Wins 80% of Beginner Games"
- Numbers, stakes, and contrast ("$1 vs $1,000,000") outperform adjectives.
  "INSANE" and "CRAZY" are dead weight — the *concept* must be insane, the
  title just states it plainly.
- Write **10 titles per video**, pick the one that makes the promise biggest
  *while staying 100% true*. Betrayed clicks destroy retention, and retention
  is the algorithm.

## Step 5 — Thumbnails

- **3 elements maximum.** A thumbnail is read in 0.4 seconds on a phone screen
  the size of a cracker. Face + object + one visual idea. Nothing else.
- The thumbnail and title must **not repeat each other** — they're two lines
  of one joke. Thumbnail shows the situation; title raises the stakes.
- Emotion on faces: real ones (fear, awe, joy), not the open-mouth default —
  that pattern is so saturated it now reads as spam to older audiences.
- Make **3 candidate thumbnails** per video. Post-launch, if CTR
  underperforms, swap to candidate #2 — YouTube lets you A/B test ("Test &
  Compare"). Use it on every upload.
- Study your own outliers monthly: your audience tells you what they click.
  Their clicks outrank any guru's rules, including these.

## The scorecard

Every video, before filming, fill out
[templates/video-scorecard.md](templates/video-scorecard.md). It forces this
whole file into a 10-minute checklist. A video that can't pass the scorecard
doesn't get made — that discipline is the entire difference between 10 years
of drift and 8 years to the crown.
