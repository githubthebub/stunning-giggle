# 03 — Retention Engineering & Building Your Format

The Idea Engine gets the click. This file keeps the viewer — and retention is
what the algorithm actually pays for. A video that holds people gets pushed to
more people; that loop, compounding across a catalog, is what a 100M-sub
channel *is*.

## The first 30 seconds (where videos live or die)

The viewer clicked a promise. The first 30 seconds have exactly one job:
**re-confirm the click.**

1. **Cold open on the promise.** First shot = the thing from the thumbnail,
   or the moment right before it. No logos, no "hey guys, welcome back," no
   channel intro — ever. Every second before the promise costs you a
   measurable % of the audience.
2. **Show the stakes in the first 15 seconds.** What's being attempted, what
   happens if it fails, why it's hard.
3. **Plant the open loop:** flash the most insane moment from later in the
   video ("...and by day 3, this happens"), then rewind to the start.

**Benchmark: 70%+ of viewers still watching at 0:30.** Below that, the hook is
broken and nothing downstream matters.

## Structure: "but / therefore," never "and then"

Weak videos are lists: this happened, and then this, and then this. Retention
graphs on those look like ski slopes.

Strong videos are chains of tension:

```
We tried X → BUT it failed because Y → THEREFORE we had to do Z → BUT Z meant...
```

Every 60–90 seconds, one of these must land:
- a **payoff** (something promised gets delivered),
- a **raise** (stakes go up), or
- a **turn** (new problem, new information).

If a 90-second stretch has none of the three, cut it. The watch-time you
"lose" by shortening is fake — a 12-minute video watched 55% beats a 20-minute
video watched 30%, in both algorithm terms and audience trust.

## Endings

- **Never** wind down with an outro, recap, or "like and subscribe" segment —
  the retention graph cliff at the end trains the algorithm that your videos
  end badly.
- Deliver the final payoff in the last 15 seconds, then hard-cut to the next
  video's tease or just end. Leave them mid-emotion.

## Analytics: the four numbers that matter

| Metric | Floor | Good | Elite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CTR (browse/suggested traffic) | 4% | 6% | 8–12% |
| Retention at 0:30 | 60% | 70% | 80%+ |
| Average % viewed | 40% | 50% | 60%+ |
| Views vs your channel median | 1x | 3x | 10x (outlier — study it) |

Every underperforming video is diagnosed with one question: **low CTR
(packaging failed) or low retention (video failed)?** They have different
fixes. The [weekly review](templates/weekly-review.md) makes this routine.

Look at the retention graph per video: every dip has a timestamp, every
timestamp is a lesson. Rewatch what you did at that moment; never do it again.

## Building your Format (the actual moat)

A **format** is a repeatable video engine: a premise pattern the audience
recognizes, escalating variables, a predictable-but-surprising payoff. Formats
are the moat because:

- Viewers who liked one instance will click the next 20 (built-in CTR).
- Production gets cheaper and better each rep (built-in quality curve).
- The format becomes synonymous with **you** (the Category-King crown).

Anatomy of the great ones — same skeleton every time:

```
[Person] attempts [specific hard thing] under [constraint], with [stakes].
Escalate one variable per episode.
```

- "Last to leave the circle wins $500K" — endurance + rising pot
- "I survived 50 hours in [place]" — place escalates
- "$1 vs $1,000,000 [thing]" — contrast ladder
- "Beating [game/skill] that's 'impossible'" — difficulty ladder

### Format discovery protocol (Phase 0's whole job)

1. Every 4–6 videos, test a distinct format candidate (packaging-first, per
   the Idea Engine).
2. A candidate earns **3 attempts**. Kill it if all three land under 2x your
   median.
3. When one attempt does **5–10x your median: stop everything.** Make the
   next 5 videos in that format, escalating one variable each time.
4. When 3 consecutive escalations hold the gains — that's your format.
   Congratulations, Phase 0 is over. Ride it for years; retire it only when
   the data (not boredom) says decline. Audiences tire of formats far, far
   slower than creators do — that asymmetry is free money.

## Personality: the part MrBeast left on the table

Spectacle scaled him, but it de-personalized his content — a known trade-off
he made deliberately. Your counter-position: **format + genuine personality.**
Viewers who come for the format but stay for *you* are the difference between
views and fans — and fans are who buy the Phase-3 product, show up to the
Phase-4 events, and follow you through a format change. In every video: real
reactions, real stakes you actually care about, your actual sense of humor.
The format gets the click; you are the retention.
